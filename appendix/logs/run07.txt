Here’s how the NDA for Cubicin (daptomycin) unfolded:

**1. Complete Response (rejection) Letter**

* **Date:** The FDA issued a Complete Response Letter for NDA 021572 on **October 26, 2001** ([FDA Access Data][1]).
* **Key Safety Concerns Cited:**

  * **Skeletal muscle toxicity:** Animal studies and early human trials showed dose-dependent elevations in creatine phosphokinase (CPK) and histologic evidence of myopathy ([PMC][2]).
  * **Neuromuscular effects:** Signals of potential neuropathy (e.g. weakness, nerve conduction changes) lacked adequate characterization.
  * **Dosing schedule question:** Twice-daily administration appeared to increase muscle toxicity; the FDA wanted data supporting safety of the proposed regimen.
  * **Insufficient human safety monitoring plan:** The protocols did not yet include a comprehensive strategy for regular CPK monitoring and clinical exams to detect muscle injury early.

**2. How Cubist Addressed the Deficiencies**
In their resubmission (early 2002), Cubist Pharmaceuticals:

* **Revised the dosing regimen** to **once-daily administration**, based on preclinical toxicokinetic modeling showing lower peak exposures and reduced muscle uptake per dose.
* **Conducted additional toxicology studies** in two species demonstrating that muscle changes were **reversible** when dosing was spaced 24 hours apart.
* **Built in robust CPK monitoring** in all pivotal clinical trials, with scheduled enzyme checks (baseline, every 3 days during therapy, and at follow-up) and standardized neurologic assessments to catch early signs of myopathy.
* **Enhanced the clinical safety database** by adding a dedicated muscle-toxicity cohort in Phase III, enrolling patients at higher risk (e.g. those on statins) and collecting long-term follow-up data.
* **Updated manufacturing controls** and stability data to ensure consistent product quality and reduce any confounding safety risks.

**3. Final Approval**

* **Date of Approval:** The FDA granted approval on **September 12, 2003** ([FDA Access Data][3]).
* **Outcome:** With the once-daily regimen, rigorous safety monitoring, and augmented toxicology package, daptomycin cleared the FDA’s remaining concerns and was launched as Cubicin for complicated skin-structure infections (and later bloodstream infections) in adults.
