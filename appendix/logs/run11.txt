RESPONSE (verbatim):

The FDA initially rejected daptomycin (Cubicin) in 2001 due to significant safety concerns, particularly related to **muscle toxicity**. The specific safety concerns cited in the FDA’s rejection letter, dated **June 28, 2001**, included:

- **Myopathy and rhabdomyolysis**: The FDA observed a high incidence of muscle-related adverse events, including elevated creatine phosphokinase (CPK) levels, myalgia (muscle pain), and clinically significant rhabdomyolysis (breakdown of muscle tissue) in clinical trial participants.
- **Dose-dependent toxicity**: The muscle toxicity appeared to be dose-dependent, with higher rates of adverse events at the higher dose levels tested (e.g., 8 mg/kg/day).
- **Lack of clear safety margin**: The FDA expressed concern that the therapeutic window was narrow, with serious muscle damage occurring at doses that were only slightly above those needed for efficacy.

These concerns were significant enough that the FDA issued a Complete Response Letter (CRL) rejecting the New Drug Application (NDA) 21-272, which had been submitted by Cubist Pharmaceuticals.

### How Cubist Addressed the Concerns in the Resubmission:

In response to the 2001 rejection, Cubist Pharmaceuticals conducted additional clinical studies and revised their dosing strategy to mitigate the safety risks. Key actions included:

1. **Reduced Dosing Regimen**: Cubist lowered the recommended dose from 8 mg/kg/day to **6 mg/kg/day**, which significantly reduced the incidence of muscle toxicity while maintaining efficacy.
2. **Enhanced Monitoring Protocols**: They implemented strict monitoring requirements, including **baseline and periodic CPK testing** during treatment, with dose adjustments or discontinuation if CPK levels rose above certain thresholds (e.g., >2.5 times the upper limit of normal).
3. **Refined Patient Selection**: The resubmission included more stringent inclusion/exclusion criteria to avoid patients at higher risk for muscle toxicity (e.g., those with pre-existing myopathy or renal impairment).
4. **Additional Clinical Data**: Cubist submitted new data from Phase III trials (e.g., the CUBICIN-001 and CUBICIN-002 trials) that demonstrated efficacy in treating complicated skin and soft tissue infections (cSSTIs) and bacteremia with a more favorable safety profile at the lower dose.

### Approval Dates:

- **Rejection Letter**: June 28, 2001  
- **Final FDA Approval**: **February 25, 2003**

The FDA approved daptomycin for the treatment of cSSTIs and bacteremia caused by susceptible Gram-positive bacteria, including methicillin-resistant *Staphylococcus aureus* (MRSA), under the revised dosing and monitoring guidelines.

This case is a notable example of how pharmaceutical companies can address serious safety concerns through dose optimization, enhanced monitoring, and robust clinical data to achieve regulatory approval.
